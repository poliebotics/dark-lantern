# ARM-I: the image-conditioned diffusion evaluator tested on previously unseen Truth Beam configurations

## Summary

ARM-I scores agreement between a recording and a supplied projected pattern. Trained on the 2026 recordings, it preferred the correct pattern over the average prescribed wrong patterns on 447 of 448 December 2024 frames, with pooled AUROC 0.657; a second seed gave 440 of 448 and 0.639. Reverse transfer also worked, with 2026 session AUROCs 0.821 and 0.766. Raw 2023 inputs did not transfer successfully. Training across all three recorded rig configurations produced AUROC 1.000 on the 2026 evaluation rows and the aligned 2023 trailer, and 0.788 / 0.726 on the held-out 2024 session. One adaptation session reached 0.948 on 2024 and 0.9998 on the aligned trailer, although the 2023 adaptation reduced d2 retention to 0.802. Target sessions were excluded from weight training, selected evaluation rows were monitored, and the trailer alignment used 25 pairs from that trailer. Controls support correspondence under several alternative negatives without establishing how much comes from spatial or colour information. These are emission-recording correspondence results within this recorded corpus. The 2026 sessions were recorded aboard CittaDel, in her wheelhouse, and the 2023 and 2024 sessions in front of the same bookshelf in another room, so the 2026 to 2024 and 2024 to 2026 transfers cross scene as well as camera, resolution and pattern family, while the 2023 to 2024 pair shares a room; a scene-held-out test with a third scene still does not exist. They establish no realness, liveness or physical-capture claim. The published ZK proofs bind the specified ARM-C execution only, and do not prove ARM-I. Both rented machines were terminated after the last pull.

## What was asked and what was done

The question set on 9 September 2026: does a conditional diffusion evaluator trained on the existing 2026 recordings hold up when tested on a previously unseen rig configuration, the older material in particular. The published zkdiff evaluator is ARM-C, a conditional diffusion U-Net (DiffusionDiagnosticUNet, width 16, 96x112 frame grid) whose conditioning is the chain's XOF octaves. The 2024 and 2023 sessions have no XOF, so the test needs the image-conditioned sibling, ARM-I: the identical architecture and recipe with the rendered emission image (area-resized to the frame grid, 3 channels, plus the 2 coordinate channels, 5 hint channels instead of 14) as the conditioning. ARM-I was trained on the 2026 data exactly as the published ARM-C candidate was, then tested, with no fine-tuning, on the seven December 2024 sessions and the eight April 2023 sessions, which were excluded from weight training (20241219_050046 and the 2023 trailer 1682718815 were monitored by the in-loop quick_eval during forward training, and d2 during reverse training; monitoring reads rows and moves no weights). A reverse arm trained ARM-I on six of the 2024 sessions and tested it on the seventh, on 2023 and on the 2026 evaluation blocks. A positive control retrained the published ARM-C conditioning with the same trainer copy and seed to confirm the copy reproduces the published numbers.

The statistic is the published one, unchanged: at diffusion timestep 150, the mean squared noise-prediction error (the residual) of a camera frame under its own emission is compared with the residual under wrong emissions of the same session at the published offsets [-2, +2, -15, +15, +30]; the pooled AUROC of the correct residual against the per-row mean wrong residual, and the paired fraction of rows whose correct residual is the lower, are reported per session and pooled per era with 1,000-resample bootstrap confidence intervals. It measures emission-recording correspondence under a change of rig, camera, alignment and emission family. It makes no realness, liveness or capture claim.

## Preprocessing, stated exactly

C, the camera frame, is the whole frame with no crop in every era. For 2026 it is the published ARM-C loader unchanged: the raw 4600x5320 RGGB frame packed to four planes (R, G, G, B at 2300x2660), divided by 255, area-resized to 96x112. For 2024 and 2023 the archived frames are demosaiced 8-bit colour images, so they are re-mosaiced onto the same RGGB grid before the same packing: R from the top-left pixel of each 2x2 cell, G from the top-right and bottom-left, B from the bottom-right, giving four planes at half resolution (2300x2660 for 2024, 768x1024 for 2023), divided by 255 and area-resized to 96x112. The 2024 `recordings` are stored BGR (the recorder's `cv2.cvtColor(captured, cv2.COLOR_BGRA2BGR)`), so the R plane is taken from channel 2 and the B plane from channel 0; the 2023 reports are RGB from gxipy's `convert("RGB")`.

E, the emission, is the rendered image, RGB in [0, 1], area-resized to 96x112: the 2026 tile PNG (1920x1080) and the 2024 HDF5 `emissions` (1920x1080 RGB, shown through pygame in RGB) are the displayed images; the 2023 emission is an emission-image approximation: the stored float array clipped to 0..255 and divided by 255 with the channel axis reversed, because the trailer's recorder wrote and re-read it through OpenCV and showed it with `cv2` (BGR order), so channel 2 of the stored array is the red the projector emitted. The real display path also passed through an 8-bit PNG and a bilinear resize to 1920x1200, which the loader does not reproduce (about 0.20/255 mean absolute difference after reduction on two sampled frames, Astra's measurement; the score impact is unmeasured), and applying the trailer's convention to the earlier 2023 recorder revisions is an inference. The 2023 aspect change (1024x1024 shown at 1920x1200, then resized to 112x96) and the 2026 one (16:9 to 112x96) are both plain area resizes of the whole image with no crop, matching the published posture.

The wrong emission for a row is another emission of the same session at the published offsets. A wrong row outside the session is skipped, never imputed. As a secondary statistic the single-wrong rule of the zkdiff proof rows is also reported: offset OFFSETS[row mod 5], mirrored when it leaves the session.

## Model and recipe

DiffusionDiagnosticUNet as in the kit (`kit/src/phase_g/diffusion_diagnostic_model.py`): base width 16, channel multipliers (1, 2, 4, 4), attention at the deepest level only, ControlNet-style hint encoder with zero-convolution adapters, scalar timestep FiLM, cosine schedule with T = 1,000, eps-prediction MSE. ARM-I differs from ARM-C only in the hint stack: [E (3), coord (2)] = 5 channels against [12 XOF octaves, coord (2)] = 14, so 1,113,204 parameters against 1,114,500. Training is the published G0e recipe verbatim (`run_g0e_sweep.sh`): from scratch, no hint dropout, 24,000 steps, micro-batch 8, no accumulation, AdamW lr 2e-4 with weight decay 0.01, 200 warm-up steps, cosine horizon 24,000, bf16 autocast, gradient clip 1.0, the splitmix64 row sampler, trainer v3 seeding before construction, OMP 4. The trainer is a copy of the kit's `train_lean.py` (sha256 7b0b5c7f...) with the conditioning switch and the old-configuration loaders added; the published kit was not edited.

The two statistics disagree in size for a plain reason that the histograms show. The absolute margin on the 2024 rig is 0.00244 against 0.02268 on pooled d2/v10, about one tenth; as a fraction of the correct residual it is 6.01 percent against 198.08 percent, about thirty-threefold, because the 2024 residual level is also four times higher. The residual level varies from frame to frame by more than the margin (the beam is 49 percent of the frame and the participant walks through it in 17 to 28 frames of every 64), and in the motion split below the preference persists in both observed motion strata. The pooled AUROC compares residuals across frames and is dragged toward chance by that between-frame spread; the paired fraction compares each frame with itself and shows that the preference for the true emission is all but universal. Both are the published protocol's statistics and both are reported.

The pairing check (the residual under the emission one row before and one row after) gives the same AUROC as the far offsets, so the archived index pairing is the right one; a one-row misalignment would have put the correct residual level with the shifted ones. The single-wrong rule of the proof rows gives 0.641 to 0.709, the same picture.

Spatial-frequency association inside 2024 (observational, not a controlled experiment: each frame's Perlin period is fixed by its hash; periods from the data-look desk's per-frame Perlin parameters (`kit/perlin/perlin_params_*.json`), mean over the three channels; camera pixels are 2.40 times these; the binning uses the correct emission's period while 1,285 of the 1,792 negative comparisons cross bins, so the matched-period control below is the controlled comparison): AUROC rises with the pattern period, 0.634 [0.621, 0.659] at 40 to 60 px (97 rows), 0.642 [0.625, 0.663] at 60 to 80 (143), 0.666 [0.649, 0.690] at 80 to 110 (157), 0.735 [0.695, 0.799] at 110 to 200 (51); paired 0.99 to 1.00 in every bin; Spearman correlation of period with the per-row margin 0.36 (seed 20260907: 0.632, 0.625, 0.644, 0.722; 0.21). Coarser patterns are associated with better transfer; the matched-period negatives in the controls section test whether the association survives when the negative has the nearest RGB-average period (maximum matching gap 16 pixels).

Two readings follow. First, alignment improved correspondence on 2023: with the recordings warped into the emission frame, the 2026-trained model prefers the true 2023 emission on 91 to 94 percent of 3,359 frames from a rig three years older with another camera and another emission family, at AUROC 0.543 to 0.553, where the raw frames were consistent with chance. The warp changes orientation, perspective, background and occupancy together, so no single cause is isolated; the measured 180 degree rotation is credible, but at the model input the native camera-pixel scales largely cancel in the resize (in the raw layouts, before any warp, the 2023 field on the frame grid is about 92 percent horizontal and 114 percent vertical of the 2024 one, both about half a frame), so scale is not the explanation. The channel-order diagnostic had shown the convention was not the barrier under the raw layout; channel sensitivity after alignment was not tested. Second, the same alignment applied to 2024 removes most of the raw-frame signal (paired 0.998 to 0.634): the raw 2024 frame already resembles the 2026 layout (upright field covering about half the frame, same sensor size), and the aligned version fills the whole grid, a layout absent from training. A third variant that places the aligned field back into a 2026-like frame layout would separate the effects and was not run (no post-hoc tuning by results).

Reading. The offset rule does not inflate the result: random and exhaustive negatives give the same AUROC and paired fraction as the prescribed offsets, and against the mean of all 63 alternatives every one of the 448 frames prefers its own emission. Identification among all candidates is a different and harder statistic and is kept distinct: 306 of 448 frames (68.3 percent; second seed 190, 42.4 percent) have a lower residual than every single one of their 63 alternatives, with no interval computed for these counts. The period control matches the negative to the nearest RGB-average Perlin period (mean gap 0.88 px, maximum 16.0 px), not identical per-channel frequencies; with it, 97.3 percent of frames still prefer the true emission at AUROC 0.653, so the observed period association does not carry the preference. The colour control rescales the wrong emission's per-channel mean and standard deviation to the correct one's and clips, which matches those two moments rather than the whole colour distribution; the preference survives it on 98.4 percent of frames. Permuting the pixels of the correct emission (same colour statistics, no spatial arrangement, and an input unlike any training input) raises the residual on 77.2 percent of frames (second seed 56.5 percent), a shuffle penalty of 26.8 percent of the correct-to-wrong gap for the main seed and 5.2 percent for the second. Together: the model shows sensitivity to spatial arrangement, and the preference survives the tested colour controls; these experiments do not partition spatial and colour contributions. Motion: splitting the 448 frames by the data-look desk's leave-one-out change fraction of the beam at its 4 percent threshold (`kit/motion_split.py`, `results/armi_2026_s20260908.motion_split.json`), the 301 static frames give AUROC 0.669 [0.656, 0.686], paired 1.000, and the 147 frames with a person or motion in the beam 0.705 [0.683, 0.734], paired 0.993, with similar mean margins (2.47e-3 and 2.37e-3; second seed 0.646 / 0.987 and 0.682 / 0.973): the preference persists in both observed strata; an observational split does not establish that motion has no effect.

## Caveats and claim boundary

This measures emission-recording correspondence under a change of rig, camera, alignment and emission family. It makes no realness, liveness or capture claim, and it says nothing about the zero-knowledge proof, which binds the XOF-conditioned ARM-C, not this image-conditioned sibling.

The 2024 forward result is two numbers that must travel together: paired fraction 0.998 and pooled AUROC 0.657. Quoting either alone misstates it. The margin on the unseen rig is about 6 percent of the residual against about 200 percent in-distribution; a model that transfers its preference but not its confidence.

Under the raw layout the 2023 recordings show no positive correspondence for any zero-shot model tested, in both directions and under the secondary statistics; that is a limit of zero-shot transfer to a rig whose recorded geometry differs (a 180 degree rotation, perspective, background and occupancy change together), with a different emission family and camera, and it is not evidence about the 2023 recordings themselves. The data-look desk's per-session homographies show the alignment is stable and the recordings do track the emissions (temporal correlation 0.70 to 0.86), and the follow-up shows the correspondence is there for a model trained on the aligned inputs (unified model, held-out trailer AUROC 1.000). Variant (b) shows both sides of the alignment: it improved correspondence on 2023 for the 2026-trained model (paired 0.909, AUROC 0.543, the same without the 79 frames used to fit the homographies), while the same alignment applied to 2024, whose raw layout already resembled the 2026 one, removes most of the raw-frame signal because the field then fills the grid, a layout absent from training. The homographies are a retrospective calibration from the target sessions' own pairs (25 for the trailer), not independent calibration data.

Sessions 1680410249 and 1680410569 have 10 rows each; their intervals are wide and carry no information beyond the pooled 2023 line. Session 1682013847 uses the 118 pairs its chain log records, not the 159/158 files on disk. The 2026 august cell (rows 600..711) is the same session as training rows 0..599 and is a held-out-rows check, not an unseen-session one; d2 and v10 carry the published 60-row guards.

Preprocessing choices that could move the old-rig numbers and were fixed from the recorder sources rather than tuned: the RGGB re-mosaic of demosaiced frames (the 2026 loader sees native Bayer planes, the old ones an interpolated re-sampling of the same grid); the 2024 BGR storage order; the 2023 emission-image approximation (channel reversal, 0..255 clip, no PNG or bilinear step). No variant was chosen by looking at results; the colour-order diagnostic above is reported as a diagnostic. Scene: the 2026 sessions (d2, v10, August) were recorded aboard CittaDel, in her wheelhouse; the 2023 and 2024 sessions were recorded in another room in front of the same bookshelf. Sources: the principal's statement of 9 September 2026, verified by the desk, and the public d2 camera preview frame 1300 (`r2:truthbeam/sessions/d2/derived/Recordings_previews/frame_001300.png`, a cropped copy on the data layer under `armi/evidence/`; the public frame's sha256 32dd9572c96275ab...), which shows the wheel, the row of blind-covered windows, the bench and the masked participant, and no bookshelf. The earlier caveat in this report and in both Astra rounds that one bookshelf appears in all three eras was wrong for 2026; the data-look desk's remark that shelving appears in the 2026 contact sheet mistook the wheelhouse windows, their blinds and the bench edge for shelving. So the 2026 to 2024 and 2024 to 2026 zero-shot transfers are cross-scene tests as well as cross-camera, cross-resolution and cross-pattern-family ones; the 2023 to 2024 pair shares a room; a scene-held-out test with a third scene still does not exist, and the camera-model identity across 2024 and 2026 is unresolved. No complete cross-era exact or near-duplicate audit of the frames is recorded.

The homography variant (b) uses an externally estimated per-session alignment from the data-look desk, so it is a test of the model on aligned inputs, not of the published whole-frame posture, and a model trained on 2026 whole frames sees a field that fills the grid instead of half of it. The Perlin-period association uses the desk's per-frame periods and is a post-hoc binning of the frozen per-row scores by the correct emission's period, with 51 rows in the top bin and most negative comparisons crossing bins; the matched-period control is the controlled form.

Publication note: the 2024-to-2026 and 2026-to-2024 transfers are stated with both statistics; the raw-2023 result, at chance under the raw layout, is published here beside them, with the recovery under alignment and the unified result.
